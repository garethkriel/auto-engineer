## Task
Level 4 practice (java.time, Optional, custom checked exception): immutable `BusinessCalendar` with `addBusinessDays`, half-open `businessDaysBetween`, `Optional` `lastBusinessDayOfMonth`, and `parseHolidays` throwing checked `HolidayFormatException`. Benchmark overlap check: compared with j01-roman, j02-wordfreq, j03-lru, j04-csv, j05-intervals, j06-lcs, j07-expr, j08-graph, j09-bank, j10-counter, j11-streams, j12-fileindex; date arithmetic over a calendar shares no core algorithm with any of them (the line-based holiday parser is a trivial ISO-date parse, not CSV).

## What worked
Tests first, including property-style consistency checks (add then subtract round-trips; `businessDaysBetween(start, addBusinessDays(start, n)) == n`). Counting in `long` to avoid `Math.abs(Integer.MIN_VALUE)`.

## What failed
One test failed on attempt 1 of 32: `2026-12-23 + 10 business days` expected 2027-01-08 but code gave 2027-01-07.

## Root cause
Defect in my hand-computed expectation, not the code: I forgot that Jan 1 is not a holiday in the test calendar, so it counts as a business day. Hand-counting the ten days (Dec 24, 28, 29, 30, 31, Jan 1, 4, 5, 6, 7) confirmed the code. The independent consistency tests passed, which backed this up.
## Rule learned
When a table-driven date expectation disagrees with the code, enumerate the days one by one against the calendar's actual holiday list before touching the code; prefer round-trip/consistency properties as an independent oracle for date arithmetic.
