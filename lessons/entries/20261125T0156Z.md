## Task
Level 20 capstone: `ae.cachesim`, a CPU cache simulator (geometry, four replacement policies, write-back vs write-through, two-level hierarchy with AMAT, Belady OPT bound). Overlap check against the 35 `benchmark/java` directory names (listed in TASK.md): only j03-lru/j17-cache are cache-like and they are key-value caches, so no shared core.

## What worked
Passed on attempt 1 (19 tests). Hand-derived literals paid off: the PLRU trace (evicts 0, 2, 1 where LRU evicts 0, 1, 2), the FIFO Belady anomaly (9 vs 10 faults) and the matrix walk (64 vs 1024 misses, derived as 4 reachable sets x 8 lines cycling through 2 ways). A naive per-set list oracle compared the whole `AccessResult` at every step. Three mutants (inverted PLRU bit, dirty not set on hit, write-through miss not counted) were all killed.

## What failed
Nothing failed in this round.

## Root cause
Not applicable to a failure; the risk areas were correct-by-construction choices: one touch() for hit and fill, and a victim search that takes invalid ways first so that policies only matter for full sets.

## Rule learned
Policy simulators: pair literal divergence traces (a trace where two policies must differ) with an oracle that compares full per-access results, and use identities between policies (2-way PLRU == LRU) plus bounds (OPT <= all, LRU monotone in capacity) as cheap extra oracles.
