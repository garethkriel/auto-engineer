## What worked
- Textbook literals (dragon-book FIRST/FOLLOW, 12-state and 10-state automata, LL(1)-not-SLR and SLR-not-LL(1) grammars) plus exhaustive all-strings checks of Earley against an independent sentence enumerator.

## What failed
- Attempt 1: 2 of 52 failed, both test defects: I expected no reduce on `+` after `E -> T .` although `+` is in FOLLOW(E) of `E -> E + T`; and asserted more than 3 distinct random sentences for a grammar with exactly two sentences.

## Root cause
Expectations written from the intuition "E ends before +/*" instead of computing FOLLOW from the grammar; a distinct-count threshold written without counting the language's size.

## Rule learned
- Derive reduce-lookahead and FOLLOW expectations by computing FOLLOW from the rules on paper first, never from what a symbol "feels like" it precedes.
- Before asserting a minimum count of distinct generated items, count the language size; set the threshold per grammar.
- Parser comparisons across algorithms should compare the error index (viable-prefix property), not the expected-token sets, which legitimately differ by detection state.
